\section{Related Work}
Bastani et al. \cite{bastani} extract tree policies from reinforcement-learning policies and verify specified properties in benchmark tasks. Their evidence motivates inspecting a path through a tree. Here the target comes from a traffic timing score, and the decision path explains the selected class. A path supplies no direct evidence of board execution.

Yuan et al. \cite{yuan} use cooperating language-model agents across a network. Wei et al. \cite{wei} propose a hierarchy in which an upper-level language model adjusts a conventional controller during incidents. These preprints motivate considering coordination and division of responsibility at different scales. At a single intersection with four numerical inputs and three actions, a small tree supports explicit predicate reporting and a bounded plan vocabulary. Its runtime footprint has yet to be measured.

The government feature describes an adaptive signal trial \cite{hk}. Wu et al. quantify potential city-scale effects using data and simulation \cite{wu}, while the FHWA manual provides engineering background on phase timing \cite{koonce}. These sources motivate the control problem and its constraints. Their results concern different units of analysis and cannot establish the prototype's queue or hardware performance.
